Citation Policy

  • Cite sources for specific claims, not for atmosphere.
  • Prefer one source at the point where it matters.
  • Do not cite sources whose bibliographic details remain unverified, except in private scaffold notes.
  • If a claim comes from the internal vault rather than a public source, either present it as the author’s synthesis or remove it from citation-dependent prose.
  • The final .qmd should use the citation style already used by the site, likely a local ref.bib plus pandoc citations.

Verified Sources

Avigad: Mathematicians In The Age Of AI

Reference:

  • Jeremy Avigad, “Mathematicians in the age of AI”, arXiv:2603.03684, https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03684.
  • Citation key: Avigad2026.

Use for:

  • AI can prove research-level theorems.
  • Mathematicians should respond to changes in mathematical practice.
  • Understanding is broader than checking.
  • AI-assisted mathematics raises practical questions about proof, explanation, and use.

Do not use for:

  • A hard prediction about the date of AI replacement.
  • Claims that AI cannot do conceptual work.

Avigad: Is Mathematics Obsolete?

Reference:

  • Jeremy Avigad, “Is Mathematics Obsolete?”, arXiv:2502.14874, https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14874.
  • Citation key: Avigad2025.

Use for:

  • Background on the value of symbolic and mathematical reasoning in the age of AI.

Do not use as a main pillar unless reread before drafting.

Klowden And Tao

Reference:

  • Tanya Klowden and Terence Tao, “Mathematical methods and human thought in the age of AI”, arXiv:2603.26524, https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26524.
  • Citation key: Klowden2026.

Use for:

  • Human-centered framing of AI in mathematics.
  • Tool use and mathematical practice.
  • Caution around broad social and educational consequences.

Do not use for:

  • Specific claims about skill atrophy unless the exact passage is checked.

Commelin, Jamnik, Ochigame, Taelman, Venkatesh

Reference:

  • Johan Commelin, Mateja Jamnik, Rodrigo Ochigame, Lenny Taelman, and Akshay Venkatesh, “Shaping the Future of Mathematics in the Age of AI”, arXiv:2603.24914, https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24914.
  • Citation key: Commelin2026.

Use for:

  • Mathematical community response to AI.
  • Values, practice, teaching, technology, and ethics.
  • Intellectual autonomy and educational concerns.
  • Tools reshaping mathematical practice.

Do not use for:

  • Strong claims about inevitable community collapse.

Alon, Bloom, Gowers, Litt, Sawin, Shankar, Tsimerman, Wang, Wood

Reference:

  • Noga Alon, Thomas F. Bloom, W. T. Gowers, Daniel Litt, Will Sawin, Arul Shankar, Jacob Tsimerman, Victor Wang, and Melanie Matchett Wood, “Remarks on the disproof of the unit distance conjecture”, arXiv:2605.20695, https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20695.
  • Citation key: Alon2026.

Use for:

  • A concrete example of AI-generated mathematical work followed by human digestion.
  • The distinction between producing a proof and producing a short, human-verified, digested argument.
  • The role of breadth, search, and patience in AI-assisted proof discovery, if supported by the paper text.

Do not use for:

  • Generalizing all AI math progress to combinatorics.

Schwer

Reference:

  • Petra Schwer, “The meaning of doing mathematics”, arXiv:2509.15998, https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.15998.
  • Citation key: Schwer2026.

Use for:

  • The question of what doing mathematics means beyond solving problems.
  • Community standards, mathematical communication, and human mathematical practice.

Do not use for:

  • Detailed technical claims about AI systems.

Gwern

Reference:

  • Gwern Branwen, “Guardian Angels: LLM Personalization for Productivity and Security”, https://gwern.net/guardian-angel.
  • Citation key: Gwern2025.

Use for:

  • The Amdahl’s-law argument that a human bottleneck limits AI-assisted productivity.
  • The economic pressure from assistant workflows toward more autonomous workflows.
  • The principal-agent framing of personalized AI.

Do not use for:

  • Mathematics-specific claims.
