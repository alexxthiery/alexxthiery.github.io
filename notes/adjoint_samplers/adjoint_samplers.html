The DDPM note describes how a diffusion model turns samples into a sampler: the drift of a reversed noising process is fitted by least-squares regression (Ho, Jain, and Abbeel 2020; Song et al. 2020). The Monte Carlo situation is the mirror image: a target \(\pi(x) = \widetilde{\pi}(x)/\mathcal{Z}_\pi\) on \(\mathbb{R}^D\) with \(\widetilde{\pi} = e^{-E}\), an unknown constant \(\mathcal{Z}_\pi\), access to \(\nabla E\), and no samples. MCMC and SMC use the energy but learn nothing reusable; diffusion models learn a reusable transport but need data.

Can one train the diffusion model of \(\pi\) from its energy alone? A recent line of work does exactly this, under names such as adjoint matching and adjoint sampling (Domingo-Enrich et al. 2024; Havens et al. 2025; G.-H. Liu et al. 2025), and these notes are my attempt to understand the simple structure underneath. The drift of the diffusion model is a conditional expectation of a functional of the future path, a delta in the option-pricing sense; its pathwise and likelihood-ratio representations differ by an integration by parts that trades samples of \(\pi\) for its score; and the conditional expectation can be learned by regression on paths the sampler generates itself. The one subtlety is that on a finite horizon the start of the process remembers its end, and most of the variety in the recent literature comes from the different ways of dealing with it.

A noise process that ends at \(\pi\)

The reference process

The reference is the OU process of the DDPM note, started at stationarity,

\[ dX_t = -\tfrac12 X_t \, dt + dW_t, \qquad X_0 \sim \pi_{\mathrm{ref}} = \mathcal{N}(0, I), \tag{1}\]

and \(\mathbb{P}\) denotes its law on paths over \([0,T]\). Contrary to the DDPM note, time runs from noise at \(t=0\) to the target at \(t=T\). With \(\alpha_s = e^{-s/2}\) and \(\sigma_s^2 = 1 - \alpha_s^2\),

\[ X_{t+s} = \alpha_s \, X_t + \sigma_s \, \mathbf{n} \tag{2}\]

for \(\mathbf{n}\sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)\) independent of \(X_t\), i.e. \(p_s(y \mid x) = \mathcal{N}(y; \alpha_s x, \sigma_s^2 I)\). For a fixed current time \(t\), I write \(\alpha \equiv \alpha_{T-t}\) and \(\sigma \equiv \sigma_{T-t}\). The process is reversible, so that \(X_t \mid X_{t+s} = y \sim \mathcal{N}(\alpha_s y, \sigma_s^2 I)\) as well, and Gaussian conditioning gives the bridges

\[ X_t \mid (X_0, X_T) = (x_0,x_T) \;\sim\; \mathcal{N} {\left( \alpha_t \, x_0 + \frac{\alpha_{T-t} \, \sigma_t^2}{\sigma_T^2} \, (x_T - \alpha_T \, x_0), \; \frac{\sigma_t^2 \, \sigma_{T-t}^2}{\sigma_T^2} \, I \right)} . \tag{3}\]

Several of the papers cited below use a Brownian reference; its transitions and bridges follow from \(\alpha_s \to 1\) and \(\sigma_s^2 \to s\).

Reweighting the end

As in the note on Doob h-transforms, reweight each path by a function of its endpoint,

\[ \frac{d\mathbb{Q}}{d\mathbb{P}}(X) = \frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}_\pi} \, \frac{\widetilde{\pi}(X_T)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(X_T)} = e^{g(X_T)} \qquad \text{with} \qquad g(x) = \log \frac{\pi(x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(x)} = -E(x) + \tfrac12 \|x\|^2 + \text{const}, \tag{4}\]

normalized by \(\mathcal{Z}_\pi\) since \(X_T \sim \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}\) under \(\mathbb{P}\), so that \(\mathbb{Q}_T = \pi\). A weight that only depends on \(X_T\) leaves the bridges unchanged, and by reversibility the reference conditioned on \(X_T = y\) is the noising process started at \(y\) and run backward. Hence \(\mathbb{Q}\) is the diffusion model of \(\pi\): draw \(X_T \sim \pi\) and run the noising process backward from it. Its marginal \(\mathbb{Q}_t\) is \(\pi\) noised for a duration \(T-t\), the law of \(\alpha_{T-t} Y + \sigma_{T-t} \mathbf{n}\) with \(Y \sim \pi\). The Doob h-transform gives the dynamics,

\[ dX_t = -\tfrac12 X_t \, dt + \textcolor{blue}{\nabla \log h(t, X_t)} \, dt + dW_t \qquad \text{with} \qquad h(t,x) = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}} {\left[ e^{g(X_T)} \mid X_t = x \right]} , \tag{5}\]

and the rest of these notes is about learning this vector field. Integrating Equation 4 over everything but \(X_t\) gives

\[ \mathbb{Q}_t(x) = \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(x) \, h(t,x), \tag{6}\]

so that the total drift in Equation 5 is \(\tfrac12 x + \nabla \log \mathbb{Q}_t(x)\), the reverse-diffusion drift of the DDPM note.

The look-ahead function

By Equation 2,

\[ h(t,x) = \int \mathcal{N}(y; \alpha x, \sigma^2 I) \, e^{g(y)} \, dy \tag{7}\]

is the weight \(e^{g} = \pi / \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}\) blurred at scale \(\sigma\) and read at the shrunk point \(\alpha x\). With \(m_\pi\) and \(C_\pi\) the mean and covariance of \(\pi\), an expansion in \(\alpha = e^{-(T-t)/2}\) gives

\[ \nabla \log h(t,x) = \alpha \, m_\pi + \alpha^2 \, (C_\pi - I) \, x + O(\alpha^3): \tag{8}\]

far from the end, the drift only knows the mean of \(\pi\), then its covariance, and finer features only appear at higher orders.

Some details:

Completing the square with \(\sigma^2 = 1 - \alpha^2\) gives \(h(t,x) = \sigma^{-D} \, e^{-\alpha^2 \|x\|^2 / (2\sigma^2)} \, \mathbb{E}_{\pi} {\left[ \exp {\left\{ \alpha \left< x,Y \right>/\sigma^2 - \alpha^2 \|Y\|^2/(2\sigma^2) \right\}} \right]} \). Differentiating, \(\nabla \log h(t,x) = \alpha {\left( \widehat{x} - \alpha x \right)} / \sigma^2\), where \(\widehat{x}\) is the mean of \(\pi\) tilted by the exponential factor. For small \(\alpha\) this tilted mean is \(m_\pi + \alpha \, C_\pi \, x + O(\alpha^2)\), and \(\sigma^2 = 1 + O(\alpha^2)\).

The marginals \(\mathbb{Q}_t\) interpolate between (nearly) \(\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}\) and \(\pi\), like the tempering path \(\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}^{1-\beta} \, \pi^{\beta}\) of annealed importance sampling and SMC (see the notes on Jarzynski and Crooks), except that they blur the target instead of flattening it: the cumulants of order \(k\) of \(\mathbb{Q}_t\) are those of \(\pi\) multiplied by \(\alpha^k\). The modes of \(\pi\) are merged early and separate only close to the end, so a diffusion sampler decides “which mode” early and “where in the mode” late, whereas tempering keeps the modes in place and lowers the barriers between them.

A perfect sampler is a perfect proposal

The path distribution \(\mathbb{Q}\) is also the zero-variance importance sampling proposal for \(\mathcal{Z}_\pi = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[\widetilde{\pi}(X_T) / \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(X_T)]\), as noted at the end of the Girsanov note. Any drift \(u\) defines a proposal \(\mathbb{P}^u\), the law of \(dX_t = -\tfrac12 X_t \, dt + u(t,X_t) \, dt + dW_t\) with \(X_0 \sim \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}\), with weights

\[ w(X) = \frac{\widetilde{\pi}(X_T)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(X_T)} \, \exp {\left\{ -\int_0^T u(t,X_t)^\top \, dW_t - \frac 12 \int_0^T \|u(t,X_t)\|^2 \, dt \right\}} , \qquad \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}^u}[w] = \mathcal{Z}_\pi, \tag{9}\]

where \(dW_t\) are the noise increments used to simulate the path. These weights debias any learned sampler (self-normalized importance sampling, or SMC with resampling (Phillips et al. 2024)), and they are constant exactly when \(\mathbb{P}^u = \mathbb{Q}\), start included.

The drift is an average

Under \(\mathbb{Q}\), conditionally on \(X_t = x\), the endpoint is distributed as

\[ \mathbb{Q}(X_T \in dy \mid X_t = x) = \frac{p_{T-t}(y \mid x) \, e^{g(y)}}{h(t,x)} \, dy \;\propto\; \pi(y) \, \mathcal{N}(x; \alpha y, \sigma^2 I) \, dy, \tag{10}\]

the posterior of a clean sample given its noisy version, exactly as in the DDPM note.

Push the endpoint

Differentiating \(h(t,x) = \mathbb{E}[ e^{g(\alpha x + \sigma \mathbf{n})} ]\) with the noise \(\mathbf{n}\) held fixed, dividing by \(h\) and using Equation 10,

\[ \nabla \log h(t,x) = \alpha \; \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}} {\left[ \nabla g(X_T) \mid X_t = x \right]} = \alpha \; \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}} {\left[ \textcolor{blue}{\nabla \log \pi(X_T) + X_T} \mid X_t = x \right]} . \tag{11}\]

The drift is the average force felt at the endpoint, transported back to the present by the factor \(\alpha = \alpha_{T-t}\) by which a nudge of \(X_t\) survives until time \(T\). This is the generalized Tweedie formula at the end of the Tweedie note, with their \(X\), \(Y\) and \(p_Y / \rho\) being our \(X_T\), \(X_t\) and \(h\).

Pull toward the endpoint

Differentiating the kernel instead, with \(\nabla_x \log p_{T-t}(y \mid x) = \alpha (y - \alpha x) / \sigma^2\),

\[ \nabla \log h(t,x) = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}} {\left[ \textcolor{blue}{\frac{\alpha \, (X_T - \alpha x)}{\sigma^2}} \;\Big|\; X_t = x \right]} = \frac{\alpha}{\sigma^2} {\left( \widehat{x}(t,x) - \alpha x \right)} \qquad \text{with} \qquad \widehat{x}(t,x) = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[X_T \mid X_t = x]. \tag{12}\]

Each endpoint the process might reach pulls it with the drift of the OU bridge pinned there, and the drift is the average pull; equivalently, the process denoises its future and moves toward the guess.

Why the two agree

The kernel depends on \(y - \alpha x\) only, so \(\nabla_x p_{T-t}(y \mid x) = -\alpha \, \nabla_y p_{T-t}(y \mid x)\) and an integration by parts moves the derivative onto the weight,

\[ \int \nabla_x p_{T-t}(y \mid x) \, e^{g(y)} \, dy = \alpha \int p_{T-t}(y \mid x) \, \nabla_y e^{g(y)} \, dy . \tag{13}\]

In option-pricing terms, \(h(t,x)\) is the price of the payoff \(e^{g(X_T)}\), \(\nabla \log h\) is a normalized delta, and Equation 11 and Equation 12 are its pathwise and likelihood-ratio estimators (Glasserman 2003). For general diffusions the link between the two is the Malliavin integration by parts used for Greeks (Fournié et al. 1999); with Gaussian transitions it is the single line Equation 13. Integrating by parts on the marginal \(\mathbb{Q}_t(x) = \int \pi(y) \, p_{T-t}(x \mid y) \, dy\) instead gives the target score identity \(\nabla \log \mathbb{Q}_t = \alpha^{-1} \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[\nabla \log \pi(X_T) \mid X_t]\) of (De Bortoli et al. 2024), hence by Equation 6 a third representation,

\[ \nabla \log h(t,x) = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}} {\left[ \textcolor{blue}{\frac{\nabla \log \pi(X_T)}{\alpha} + x} \;\Big|\; X_t = x \right]} . \tag{14}\]

Samples or scores

Any \(\xi_t\) with \(\mathbb{E}[\xi_t \mid X_t] = \nabla \log h(t, X_t)\) turns the drift into a least-squares problem, for any time weighting \(\omega(t) > 0\),

\[ \nabla \log h = \mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{u} \; \int_0^T \omega(t) \; \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}} \big\| u(t, X_t) - \xi_t \big\|^2 \, dt, \tag{15}\]

and Equation 11 and Equation 12 provide the integrands

\[ \xi^{\text{push}}_t = \textcolor{blue}{\alpha_{T-t} \, {\left( \nabla \log \pi(X_T) + X_T \right)} } \qquad \text{and} \qquad \xi^{\text{pull}}_t = \textcolor{blue}{\frac{\alpha_{T-t} \, (X_T - \alpha_{T-t} X_t)}{\sigma_{T-t}^2}}. \tag{16}\]

The pull regression needs endpoints distributed as \(\pi\) and nothing else: it is denoising score matching (Vincent 2011), the way diffusion models are trained. The push regression needs \(\nabla \log \pi\) at whatever endpoints it is given: it is the regression behind adjoint matching and adjoint sampling (Domingo-Enrich et al. 2024; Havens et al. 2025). Samples tell us where \(\pi\) is, the score tells us which way is uphill, and an integration by parts trades one for the other. The regression itself is least-squares Monte Carlo in the sense of Longstaff and Schwartz (Longstaff and Schwartz 2001): it amortizes over all states what particle methods estimate state by state (Phillips et al. 2024).

The integrands have the same conditional mean and very different fluctuations. The push integrand is \(O(\alpha)\), bounded close to the end, but it inherits the roughness of \(\nabla \log \pi\) (stiff molecular energies require gradient clipping (Havens et al. 2025)). The pull integrand has variance of order \(\alpha^2\) far from the end but blowing up like \(1/\sigma^2\) as \(t \to T\). The integrand of Equation 14 is \(O(1/\alpha)\), with a variance growing like \(e^{T-t}\) far from the end. This suggests pulling early and pushing late. Any mixture \(\lambda_t \, \xi^{\text{push}}_t + (1-\lambda_t) \, \xi^{\text{pull}}_t\) is a valid integrand, the difference of two integrands being a control variate; the family of integrands indexed by a function \(c(t)\) in (Blessing et al. 2026) is this freedom.

Pulling yourself up by your own samples

Both regressions average over \(\mathbb{Q}\) and therefore require endpoints \(X_T \sim \pi\). Only the endpoints matter, though: given \(X_T\), a path of \(\mathbb{Q}\) is a reference bridge, so a training pair is a stored endpoint and one Gaussian draw \(X_t = \alpha_{T-t} X_T + \sigma_{T-t} \mathbf{n}\), and no backpropagation through an SDE solver is ever needed (Havens et al. 2025). The missing endpoints are replaced by those of the current model, stored with their gradients in a replay buffer:

The algorithm in a few lines: 
repeat
    simulate K paths of dX = (-X/2 + u_theta(t, X)) dt + dW from X_0 ~ N(0, I)    # no gradient needed
    add the pairs (X_T, grad log pi(X_T)) to the buffer                          # K energy gradients
    repeat M times                                                               # M >> K
        draw (X_T, G) from the buffer, t ~ Unif[0, T] and n ~ N(0, I)
        set X_t = alpha_{T-t} X_T + sigma_{T-t} n
        take a gradient step on  omega(t) * | u_theta(t, X_t) - alpha_{T-t} (G + X_T) |^2

Let \(\rho\) be the distribution of the current endpoints and \(\mathbb{Q}^{\rho}\) the reference reweighted by \((\rho / \pi_{\mathrm{ref}})(X_T)\), the diffusion model of \(\rho\), with look-ahead function \(h^{\rho}\). The training pairs are pairs of \(\mathbb{Q}^{\rho}\), and splitting \(\log(\pi / \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}) = \log(\rho / \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}) + \log(\pi / \rho)\) in Equation 11 shows that one round of regression returns

\[ u_{\text{new}}(t,x) = \nabla \log h^{\rho}(t,x) \; + \; \textcolor{blue}{\alpha \, \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}^{\rho}} {\left[ \nabla \log \frac{\pi}{\rho}(X_T) \;\Big|\; X_t = x \right]} }: \tag{17}\]

the diffusion model of the current output, corrected toward \(\pi\). If the model is the diffusion model of its own output, \(u = \nabla \log h^{\rho}\), it is a fixed point only if the correction vanishes for all \((t,x)\); letting \(t \to T\) gives \(\nabla \log(\pi/\rho) = 0\), i.e. \(\rho = \pi\). This is the condition under which uniqueness is established in (Havens et al. 2025); I do not know of a general argument without it, although in a Gaussian toy example I found no other fixed point. With the stationary start of Equation 1 and a finite horizon, even \(\rho = \pi\) is not exactly reproduced, since the paths start at \(\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}\) instead of \(\mathbb{Q}_0\); the next section is about this. With the pull integrand, the same computation returns \(\nabla \log h^{\rho}\) exactly: every \(\rho\) is a fixed point and \(\pi\) never enters. The score is the only channel through which the target enters, and a mixture with weight \(\lambda_t\) on the push integrand multiplies the correction in Equation 17 by \(\lambda_t\).

This is adaptive importance sampling in the spirit of the cross-entropy method (Rubinstein and Kroese 2004), with a regression instead of a weighted fit and no weights. Consequently, one only learns where one looks: in Equation 17 the target is probed only at the model’s own endpoints. Far from the end, the conditional law of \(X_T\) given \(X_t\) under \(\mathbb{Q}^\rho\) is close to \(\rho\), so that \(u_{\text{new}}(t,x) \approx \alpha \, (\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[\nabla \log \pi(Y)] + \mathbb{E}_{\rho}[Y])\): everything the early “which mode” stage learns about \(\pi\) is the Stein moment \(\mathbb{E}_\rho[\nabla \log \pi]\), which vanishes for \(\rho = \pi\) and can be small when \(\rho\) misses a mode altogether. The usual remedies for mode-seeking apply: broad references, long horizons, and the weights Equation 9. Damped updates \((1-\lambda) \, u_{\text{old}} + \lambda \, u_{\text{new}}\), equivalent to a proximal penalty \(\eta \, \mathbb{E}\|u - u_{\text{old}}\|^2\) with \(\lambda = 1/(1+\eta)\), are reported to help preserve modes (Blessing et al. 2026).

The start remembers the end

The recipe so far has three dials: the integrand, the way the start and the end of the reference are tied to the prior and to \(\pi\), and the reference process itself. This section turns the second dial, which a subtlety forces upon us; the next section turns the third.

The leak

On a finite horizon, \(\mathop{\mathrm{Cov}}(X_0, X_T) = \alpha_T \, I\) with \(\alpha_T = e^{-T/2}\), so reweighting the end also reweights the start, as in the Doob note. By Equation 6,

\[ \mathbb{Q}_0(x_0) = \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(x_0) \, h(0, x_0), \tag{18}\]

the distribution \(\pi\) noised for a duration \(T\) rather than \(\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}\). The algorithm of the previous section starts its paths from \(\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}\): even with the exact drift, it simulates \(\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}(dX) = \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(dX_0) \, \mathbb{Q}(dX \mid X_0)\), and since the two path distributions share their dynamics given the start,

\[ \frac{d\mathbb{Q}}{d\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}}(X) = h(0, X_0). \tag{19}\]

The leak is the part of the importance weights that no drift can remove. The path weights Equation 9 remove it for any \(T\); without them it is a bias, with endpoint distribution

\[ \widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}_T(y) = \pi(y) \; \mathbb{E} {\left[ \frac{1}{h(0, \alpha_T \, y + \sigma_T \, \mathbf{n})} \right]} = \pi(y) \, \Big( 1 - e^{-T} \, \left< m_\pi, y - m_\pi \right> + O(e^{-3T/2}) \Big). \tag{20}\]

Some details:

Write \(\widetilde{\mathbb{Q}}_T(y) = \int \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(x_0) \, p_T(y \mid x_0) \, e^{g(y)} / h(0,x_0) \, dx_0\) and use reversibility, \(\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(x_0) \, p_T(y \mid x_0) = \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y) \, p_T(x_0 \mid y)\), together with \(\pi_{\mathrm{ref}} \, e^{g} = \pi\). For the expansion, the computation behind Equation 8 gives \(h(0,x_0) = 1 + \alpha_T \left< x_0, m_\pi \right> + \alpha_T^2 \, q(x_0) + O(\alpha_T^3)\) for a quadratic \(q\) with \(\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}[q] = 0\), since \(\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}[h(0,X_0)] = 1\) for every \(T\). Inverting and averaging over \(x_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(\alpha_T y, \sigma_T^2 I)\), the first-order term contributes \(-\alpha_T^2 \left< m_\pi, y \right>\), its square contributes \(\alpha_T^2 \|m_\pi\|^2\), and \(q\) only contributes at higher order.

To leading order, the unweighted sampler has mean \(m_\pi - e^{-T} C_\pi \, m_\pi\), pulled toward the mean of the reference, and the residual weights have effective sample size \(1 - e^{-T} \|m_\pi\|^2\). Centering the reference at a rough estimate of \(m_\pi\) removes the leading term, and the leak becomes \(O(e^{-2T})\); in a one-dimensional check, matching the covariance as well brings it down to \(O(e^{-3T})\). Diffusion models live with the same mismatch when they start their reverse process from \(\mathcal{N}(0,I)\) instead of the noised data; in the control literature it is the initial value function bias (Domingo-Enrich et al. 2024).

One formula for all ties

Let \(\Gamma(x_0, y)\) be any distribution of the endpoints whose first marginal is the prior \(p_0\) from which the model is started, and fill in the paths with reference bridges. The Markov diffusion with the same marginals has drift \(-\tfrac12 x + u(t,x)\) with

\[ u(t,x) = \alpha_{T-t} \; \mathbb{E} {\left[ \nabla_y \log w(X_0, y) \big|_{y = X_T} \;\Big|\; X_t = x \right]} \qquad \text{where} \qquad w(x_0, y) = \frac{\Gamma(x_0, y)}{p_0(x_0) \, p_T(y \mid x_0)} \tag{21}\]

is the weight of the endpoints relative to the reference. Reweighting only the end, as above, corresponds to \(w = e^{g(y)} / h(0,x_0)\), and the start factor drops out of the gradient. As \(T \to \infty\), the reference forgets its start and each of the weights below tends to \(\pi(y)/\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y)\), up to a factor of \(x_0\): all constructions become the diffusion model of \(\pi\), and the leak vanishes. On a finite horizon there are three natural choices of \(w\), three ways of paying for the finite horizon: the first is the simplest, the second stays closest to the reference, the third is fully explicit.

Some details:

The bridge density at time \(t\) is \(p_t(x \mid x_0) \, p_{T-t}(y \mid x) / p_T(y \mid x_0)\), so \((X_t, X_T)\) has density \(p_{T-t}(y \mid x) \, W(x,y)\) with \(W(x,y) = \int \Gamma(x_0, y) \, p_t(x \mid x_0) / p_T(y \mid x_0) \, dx_0\). The drift is the average bridge drift (see Equation 26 below), whose non-trivial part is \(\int \nabla_x p_{T-t}(y \mid x) \, W(x,y) \, dy / \Pi_t(x)\); the integration by parts Equation 13 in \(y\) turns it into \(\alpha_{T-t} \, \mathbb{E}[\nabla_y \log W(x, X_T) \mid X_t = x]\). Finally, \(\nabla_y \log W(x,y)\) is the average of \(\nabla_y \log[\Gamma(X_0, y) / p_T(y \mid X_0)]\) given \((X_t, X_T) = (x,y)\), and the tower property removes the inner conditioning.

Forget the start

If the start and the end of the reference are independent, \(h(0,\cdot)\) is constant and nothing leaks. One option is a long horizon. Another is to start the reference from a point, \(X_0 = 0\), as in (Havens et al. 2025): the reference then ends at \(p_T(\cdot \mid 0) = \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_T^2 I)\), the weight is \(w = \pi(y) / p_T(y \mid 0)\), and the construction is exact for any horizon, with integrand

\[ \xi^{\text{push}}_t = \alpha_{T-t} {\left( \nabla \log \pi(X_T) + \frac{X_T}{\sigma_T^2} \right)} \tag{22}\]

and training pairs drawn from Equation 3 with \(x_0 = 0\). With a Brownian reference and \(T = 1\), \(\mathbb{Q}\) is the Föllmer process, with integrand \(\nabla \log \pi(X_1) + X_1\).

A pretrained generative model cannot be started from a point, but its noise schedule can be chosen so that it forgets its start: the memoryless schedule of (Domingo-Enrich et al. 2024) is an OU process run for an infinite time on a squeezed clock. For a model built on the interpolation \(X_t = a_t Y + b_t \mathbf{n}\) between noise (\(a_0 = 0\), \(b_0 = 1\)) and data \(Y\) (\(a_1 = 1\), \(b_1 = 0\)), the rescaled \(Z_t = X_t / \sqrt{a_t^2 + b_t^2}\) has the OU transitions Equation 2 from \(Y\) after an OU time \(\tau_t = \log[(a_t^2 + b_t^2)/a_t^2]\), which diverges as \(t \to 0\). The time reversal of this noising process has diffusion coefficient \(\sigma_t^2 = 2 \eta_t\) with \(\eta_t = b_t (\dot a_t b_t / a_t - \dot b_t)\), the memoryless schedule; for flow matching, \(\sigma_t^2 = 2(1-t)/t\).

Some details:

For \(t < t'\), the noising process satisfies \(X_t \mid X_{t'} \sim \mathcal{N} {\left( \frac{a_t}{a_{t'}} X_{t'}, {\left( b_t^2 - \frac{a_t^2}{a_{t'}^2} b_{t'}^2 \right)} I \right)} \). After rescaling, \(Z_t \mid Z_{t'}\) is Gaussian with mean coefficient \(e^{-(\tau_t - \tau_{t'})/2}\) and variance \(1 - e^{-(\tau_t - \tau_{t'})}\), the OU transition over an OU time \(\tau_t - \tau_{t'}\). The squared diffusion coefficient of the noising process in the variable \(t\) is \(-\frac{d}{dt} b_t^2 + 2 \frac{\dot a_t}{a_t} b_t^2 = 2 \eta_t\), and a time reversal keeps it.

Tilt the start

Keep any prior \(p_0\) (a harmonic prior for molecules, or \(\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}\) at finite \(T\)) and reweight both ends, as for Schrödinger bridges:

\[ \frac{d\mathbb{Q}}{d\mathbb{P}}(X) \propto e^{g_0(X_0) + g(X_T)} \qquad \text{with} \qquad \mathbb{Q}_0 = p_0 \quad \text{and} \quad \mathbb{Q}_T = \pi. \tag{23}\]

A factor of \(X_0\) changes the law of the start but not the drift, so everything above applies with the push integrand \(\alpha_{T-t} \nabla g(X_T)\). The start constraint forces \(e^{g_0} = 1/h(0,\cdot)\), which cancels the leak. The end constraint forces \(e^{g} = \pi / \psi\) with

\[ \psi(y) = \int p_0(x_0) \, e^{g_0(x_0)} \, p_T(y \mid x_0) \, dx_0, \tag{24}\]

the distribution produced at time \(T\) by the reference once its start has been tilted; \(e^{g_0}\) and \(e^{g}\) are the Schrödinger potentials of the SB note, and \(\psi = p^{\mathrm{ref}}_T \, \widehat{\varphi}_T\). When the reference forgets its start, \(g_0\) is constant and \(\psi\) is the endpoint distribution of the reference. Otherwise \(\psi\) is unknown, but its score is Tweedie’s formula from the start to the end,

\[ \nabla \log \psi(y) = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}} {\left[ \nabla_y \log p_T(y \mid X_0) \mid X_T = y \right]} = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}} {\left[ -\frac{y - \alpha_T X_0}{\sigma_T^2} \;\Big|\; X_T = y \right]} , \tag{25}\]

a second regression on endpoint pairs. A model started from \(p_0\) with the drift of the current \(g\) has path distribution \(\mathbb{P}\) tilted by \(e^{g}\) at the end and by \(1/h(0,\cdot)\) at the start, so the start constraint holds for free; refitting \(\psi\) on its endpoint pairs updates \(g = \log(\pi/\psi)\). This alternation is the Sinkhorn algorithm of the SB note on path-space, used in (G.-H. Liu et al. 2025) and in the discrete setting of (Guo et al. 2026); with samples instead of scores it is the diffusion Schrödinger bridge of (De Bortoli et al. 2021). The correction fades with the memory of the reference: by Equation 25, \(\nabla \log \psi(y) + y = (\alpha_T \, \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[X_0 \mid X_T = y] - \alpha_T^2 \, y) / \sigma_T^2\), which is \(O(e^{-T/2})\), and \(O(e^{-T})\) for a centered prior.

Pair the ends independently

Finally, take \(\Gamma = p_0 \otimes \pi\), i.e. \(w = \pi(y) / p_T(y \mid x_0)\). The resulting path distribution \(\Pi\) reweights the reference by a function of both endpoints that does not factorize, and it is not Markov; the Markov diffusion with the average bridge drift

\[ b(t,x) = -\tfrac12 x + \mathbb{E}_{\Pi} {\left[ \nabla_x \log p_{T-t}(X_T \mid x) \mid X_t = x \right]} \tag{26}\]

has the same marginals (Brunick and Shreve 2013), and Equation 21 makes it explicit:

\[ b(t,x) + \tfrac12 x = \alpha_{T-t} \; \mathbb{E}_{\Pi} {\left[ \textcolor{blue}{\nabla \log \pi(X_T) + \frac{X_T - \alpha_T X_0}{\sigma_T^2}} \;\Big|\; X_t = x \right]} . \tag{27}\]

There is no second regression and no alternation: where the Schrödinger construction learns \(\nabla \log \psi\), the transition score appears inside the average. Current start and end samples are paired at random, and \(X_t\) is drawn from the bridge given both. This is the route of (Blessing et al. 2026); with samples instead of scores, it is bridge matching, or flow matching with independent coupling (Lipman et al. 2022; Albergo, Boffi, and Vanden-Eijnden 2023; X. Liu et al. 2022; Peluchetti 2023). The price is the least economical coupling of the three: \(\Pi\) is not the path distribution closest to the reference, and the learned process only matches it one time at a time.

How nudges travel

The reference enters Equation 11 only through the way a nudge at time \(t\) propagates to the endpoint, and it enters the algorithm through two conveniences: explicit bridges and an explicit endpoint distribution.

Linear references, and paths as states

For \(dX_t = -A X_t \, dt + C \, dW_t\) with the control entering as \(C u\), a nudge travels through the matrix \(e^{-A(T-t)}\) and

\[ \nabla \log h(t,x) = e^{-A^\top (T-t)} \; \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}} {\left[ \nabla g(X_T) \mid X_t = x \right]} , \qquad u^\star = C^\top \nabla \log h, \tag{28}\]

each eigenmode of a symmetric \(A\) forgetting at its own rate. This matters when the unknown is itself a discretized path, for instance a transition path of a molecule between two metastable states: with \(A\) minus the discrete Dirichlet Laplacian, the reference favours smooth paths, satisfies the boundary conditions by construction, and forgets its high-frequency modes fastest. With a point start, this is the construction of (Park, Lee, and Liu 2025), whose infinite-dimensional formulation is the limit of fine discretizations.

A pretrained model as reference

To fine-tune a pretrained diffusion or flow model \(dX_t = b(t, X_t) \, dt + \sigma_t \, dW_t\) toward a reward \(r\), run it with a memoryless schedule and reweight its paths by \(e^{r(X_T)}\) (Domingo-Enrich et al. 2024). The unknown endpoint distribution \(p_T\) of the reference cancels from this weight, whereas sampling a given \(\pi\) would require the weight \(\pi/p_T\); nonlinear references are therefore suited to fine-tuning. With the noise held fixed, nudges travel through the Jacobian \(J_{t \to T} = \partial X_T / \partial X_t\) of the flow, and

\[ \nabla \log h(t,x) = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}} {\left[ a(t) \mid X_t = x \right]} \qquad \text{with} \qquad a(t) = J_{t \to T}^\top \, \nabla r(X_T), \quad \dot{a}(t) = -\nabla b(t, X_t)^\top a(t). \tag{29}\]

This \(a(t)\) solves the adjoint equation of the adjoint method note, and it is what (Domingo-Enrich et al. 2024) call the lean adjoint. The identity Equation 29 is exact: the derivative is taken along the reference flow inside a \(\mathbb{Q}\)-average, so derivatives of the control never appear, and there is no need to start from the adjoint of the controlled flow and drop terms that vanish at the optimum. For the OU reference, \(a(t) = \alpha_{T-t} \nabla r(X_T)\) and Equation 11 is recovered. The price of a nonlinear reference is the absence of explicit bridges: whole trajectories must be simulated and stored, \(a(t)\) computed along each by vector-Jacobian products, and the endpoint buffer is lost.

Jumps

For a continuous-time Markov chain with rate matrix \(R_t(x,y)\) for jumps \(x \to y\) (the \(Q_t\) of the note on discrete diffusions), the Doob h-transform multiplies the rates,

\[ R_t(x,y) \; \frac{h(t,y)}{h(t,x)}, \tag{30}\]

and gradients become ratios. On a group such as \(\mathbb{Z}_N^D\) with translation-invariant noise, shifting the present by \(y - x\) shifts the endpoint by \(y - x\), which gives the discrete push representation (Guo et al. 2026),

\[ \frac{h(t,y)}{h(t,x)} = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}} {\left[ e^{g(X_T + y - x) - g(X_T)} \mid X_t = x \right]} . \tag{31}\]

When \(x\) and \(y\) differ in one coordinate, only single-coordinate ratios of \(\pi\) are needed. Without a group structure, as for masked diffusion, one can use the product of two independent averages,

\[ \frac{h(t,y)}{h(t,x)} = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}} {\left[ e^{g(X_T)} \mid X_t = y \right]} \times \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}} {\left[ e^{-g(X_T)} \mid X_t = x \right]} , \tag{32}\]

which is the discrete adjoint of (So et al. 2026); the backward equation they derive for it is the backward Kolmogorov equation satisfied by \(h\). Rates must stay positive, so the squared loss is replaced by a Bregman divergence such as the generalized Kullback–Leibler divergence; the conditional expectation remains the minimizer for any Bregman loss (Banerjee, Guo, and Wang 2005). The tilted rates inherit the sparsity of the reference, and tilting the start carries over with the same two devices applied to \(\psi\) (Guo et al. 2026).

Where did the stochastic control go?

By the Girsanov theorem, as in the HJB note, for a controlled process \(\mathbb{P}^u\) started like \(\mathbb{P}\),

\[ D_{\text{KL}} {\left( \mathbb{P}^u \,\|\, \mathbb{Q} \right)} = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}^u} {\left[ \frac 12 \int_0^T \|u(t,X_t)\|^2 \, dt - g(X_T) \right]} + \log \mathcal{Z}, \tag{33}\]

so \(\mathbb{Q}\) solves the control problem \(\max_u \mathbb{E}[g(X_T) - \tfrac12 \int_0^T \|u\|^2 \, dt]\), with Bellman control \(u^\star = \nabla V\) and \(V = \log h\). If the initial distribution is optimized as well, the optimum is \(p_0 \, h(0,\cdot)/\mathcal{Z}\), which is the leak of the previous sections. Earlier diffusion samplers minimize Equation 33 directly, differentiating through simulated trajectories (Zhang and Chen 2021; Vargas, Grathwohl, and Doucet 2023): variational inference with a reverse Kullback–Leibler divergence, and a simulation and a backward pass at every step.

The methods discussed here never minimize Equation 33. Kullback–Leibler control is linearly solvable (Kappen 2005; Todorov 2009): \(h\) solves the linear equation \((\partial_t + \mathcal{L}) h = 0\) of the Doob note, and \(V = \log h\) solves the HJB equation only through the Cole–Hopf transform. The optimal control is thus the gradient of the logarithm of an expectation, which Equation 11 writes as a conditional expectation and Equation 15 turns into least squares; hence the name matching. The name adjoint comes from the costate: \(a(t)\) in Equation 29, equal to \(\alpha_{T-t} \nabla g(X_T)\) for the OU reference, is Pontryagin’s costate along a path with frozen noise, and Equation 11 reads

\[ u^\star(t,x) = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}} {\left[ a(t) \mid X_t = x \right]} , \]

the stochastic maximum principle (Bismut 1973; Peng 1990) in one line. Bellman’s \(\nabla V\), Pontryagin’s conditional costate and Doob’s \(\nabla \log h\) are the same vector field, and adjoint matching regresses the control onto the costate.

Mental models to keep

  • Reweighting a noise process by where it ends gives the diffusion model of \(\pi\).
  • It blurs \(\pi\) where annealing would temper it: modes are decided early.
  • The drift is a delta: push the endpoint back, or pull toward it.
  • Samples say where \(\pi\) is; the score says which way is uphill.
  • Averages are regressions: train on your own endpoints, redraw the middle from bridges.
  • Training on your own samples is unweighted adaptive importance sampling: one only learns where one looks.
  • On a finite horizon the start remembers the end; even the exact drift leaves weights \(h(0, X_0)\).
  • Forget the start, tilt it, or pair the ends independently: three choices of one endpoint weight.
  • A different reference changes how nudges travel: a number, a matrix, a Jacobian, a shift.
  • Bellman, Pontryagin and Doob describe the same vector field; nothing is optimized but a least-squares fit.

Things I would try, none of them tested: center and whiten the reference at crude estimates of the mean and covariance of \(\pi\); pull early and push late, always keeping some push; correct learned samplers with SMC on the path weights, which also removes the leak; and prefer a moderately long OU horizon to a point start when the prior matters.
